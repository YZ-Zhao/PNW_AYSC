Classes & Curriculum

Youth Sailing Syllabus  •  Ages 8–14  •  4 Classroom + 4 On-Water Sessions

Overview

Four short lessons build the knowledge sailors need; four on-water sessions put it into practice right away. Built for ages 8–14 — clear language, hands-on drills, safety first.
Ages 8–14 4 Classroom Sessions 4 On-Water Sessions No Experience Required

Learning Goals

  • Name the parts of a sailboat and use basic sailing terms.
  • Follow water-safety practices and read basic weather cues.
  • Read wind direction and points of sail and adjust sail trim.
  • Tack and jibe with clear crew commands.
  • Apply right-of-way and collision-avoidance rules on the water.

Class Details

Boat & Safety Basics

This class covers the fundamentals every sailor needs before stepping into the boat. Sailors will learn to identify the major parts of a sailboat, tie the handful of knots used most often on the water, and read basic weather cues like wind and cloud patterns before heading out. The class also introduces how to rig the boat from the ground up and the clear, calm communication habits that keep everyone safe once they're on the water.

  • Boat anatomy
  • Lines & knots
  • Reading the weather
  • Rigging the boat
  • Communication

Points of Sail

This class introduces how sailboats move relative to the wind, which is the foundation for everything else in the program. Sailors will learn how to find true wind direction, understand why the no-go zone makes some directions impossible to sail directly into, and work through each of the five points of sail one at a time. Along the way, they'll pick up the trim rule for adjusting sails and practice calling out wind direction clearly to their crew.

  • How to find the wind
  • The no-go zone
  • The five points of sail
  • The trim rule
  • Calling out wind direction
  • Learning every point of sail

Tacking & Jibing

This class breaks down the two maneuvers sailors use to change direction — tacking and jibing — and why getting them right matters for both speed and safety. Sailors will focus on clear crew communication and readiness before each maneuver, understand how roles are divided between crew members, and learn how to control the boom safely as the boat turns through the wind. A dry-land walkthrough lets sailors practice the sequence of steps before trying it on the water.

  • Communication & readiness
  • Crew roles
  • Controlling the boom
  • Dry-land walkthrough

Rules of the Road & Final Review

This final class covers the right-of-way rules and collision-avoidance skills sailors need to safely share the water with other boats. Sailors will learn how sail and power vessels interact under right-of-way rules, work through realistic on-water scenarios to practice avoiding collisions, and revisit the points of sail covered earlier in the program to reinforce those skills. By the end of this class, sailors should feel confident applying what they've learned across a full day on the water.

  • Right of way
  • Review points of sail
  • Avoiding collision
  • Scenario practice
  • Sail versus power

Safety & What to Bring

  • Sessions may shift depending on weather, water, or equipment conditions.
  • Bring: water bottle, weather-ready clothing, and sun protection.
